# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring decision-makers and prospective clients evaluating Pedro Garcia, a fullstack
developer. The primary audience is **technical** — engineering leads, CTOs, and senior
developers who read the stack, judge how things are built, and can tell craft from
decoration at a glance. They arrive skeptical and time-poor: they want to confirm
quickly that this person can design *and* ship complete web products, then find a clear
path to start a conversation. The site is bilingual (English / Brazilian Portuguese), so
both audiences are served in their own language.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio that earns Pedro both **full-time roles and freelance/contract
work** — the two are weighted roughly equally, so the site must read as credible to a
hiring panel and approachable to a prospective client at the same time. It exists to
demonstrate end-to-end capability (database schema to pixel-perfect UI) through the work
itself, not through claims. Success is a technical visitor leaving convinced of the depth
of the craft and taking the next step — email, resume, or a project deep-dive.

## Brand Personality

**Sharp · precise · technical.** Engineer-forward and exacting. The voice is confident
without bragging — it states what was built and lets the detail carry the weight. Tone is
direct, concrete, and free of marketing fluff; specifics over adjectives. The current
dark, terminal-adjacent system (mono labels, measured motion, restrained accents) is the
right register for this personality: it should feel like it was made *by* the kind of
engineer it's trying to represent.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI / template look.** No cookie-cutter card grids, no tracked-uppercase
  eyebrow above every section, no gradient text, no numbered `01 / 02 / 03` scaffolding by
  reflex. If it looks generated, it has failed.
- **Corporate / SaaS-y.** No sterile committee aesthetic, no hero-metric template, no
  stock-photo polish. The site should have a point of view.
- **Flashy / overdesigned.** No neon overload or effect-for-effect's-sake. Motion and
  visual richness must earn their place; restraint reads as senior.
- **Cluttered / busy.** No dense walls of text, no airless sections. Hierarchy and
  breathing room are non-negotiable.

## Design Principles

- **Show, don't tell.** Capability is proven through the work and the build quality of the
  site itself, never asserted with adjectives. The portfolio is the proof.
- **Practice what you preach.** A fullstack developer's site must itself be exemplary —
  fast, accessible, responsive, bug-free. Any rough edge undermines the whole pitch.
- **Expert confidence through restraint.** Say less, mean more. The senior signal is what
  is left out as much as what is shown; resist decoration.
- **Specifics over claims.** Concrete details — real stacks, real outcomes, real
  problems solved — beat vague superlatives every time, especially for a technical reader.
- **Bilingual parity.** English and Portuguese are first-class equals; neither reads as a
  machine-translated afterthought.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target **WCAG 2.1 AA**. Body text ≥ 4.5:1 contrast (≥ 3:1 for large text) — verify against
the dark tinted surfaces, where muted ink on near-black is the likely failure point.
Full keyboard navigation with visible focus states, semantic landmarks and heading order,
and a genuine `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative for every animation (the particle
background, glow drift, and reveal transitions in particular). Don't rely on the cyan
accent alone to carry meaning.
